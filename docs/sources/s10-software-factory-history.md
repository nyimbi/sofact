## Precis

Background on the term "software factory", which predates LLMs by decades.
Recurring cycle: (1) capital-intensive, specialised, tightly-scoped production
lines (Bemer 1968 / Hitachi / Toshiba / NEC software factories of the 1970s-80s);
(2) process-standardised internal factories (Microsoft's 2004 reorganisation);
(3) the current wave, framed as "Factory 2.0", where the specialised line is
replaced by general-purpose models plus heavy verification scaffolding.

## Key claims

- Every prior wave failed on the same axis: the cost of *maintaining* the
  specialised production apparatus exceeded the value it produced.
- The current wave claims to escape this because the "machines" are general and
  the specialisation lives in prompts/oracles rather than bespoke tooling.

## Why it matters for sofact

The single most important historical lesson for a one-person local factory: heavy
bespoke scaffolding is the documented failure mode. This argues for a small
number of generic, well-instrumented lanes over a large taxonomy of specialised
agents — and for treating the factory's own maintenance cost as a first-class
metric from day one.

## Concerns / information quality

- "Factory 2.0" is marketing framing; the history is well-attested but the
  analogy may be doing persuasive work rather than analytic work.
